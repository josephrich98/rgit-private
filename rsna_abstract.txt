Purpose: Radiogenomic "virtual biopsy" is increasingly proposed to stratify renal masses non-invasively and spare patients biopsy. We asked how much of the tumor transcriptome any CT-based model could recover in clear-cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC), and whether reported radiogenomic signal reflects tumor biology or patient demographics.

Materials and Methods: 190 patients with contrast-enhanced CT and tumor RNA-seq from the multi-institutional Cancer Genome Atlas Kidney Renal Clear Cell Carcinoma (TCGA-KIRC) collection were analyzed (8 contributing institutions; 3 CT manufacturers; 29 scanner models; median age 59 years; 65 women; 57/154 staged III-IV). Treating the image as a noisy readout of the tumor genome gives a closed-form ceiling - the highest R2 any decoder could achieve for any genomic score - estimated by canonical correlation. Because that statistic inflates when feature count approaches sample size, we calibrated it by planting known correlations into permuted real data and inverting, giving a 95% upper confidence bound. We separately measured the out-of-sample R2 achievable for each of 2000 genes, whose average is the share of transcriptome variance recoverable. Four CT representations were tested (tumor and kidney pyradiomics; tumor and whole-volume RadImageNet deep features), with an a-priori ccRCC panel, size-matched random panels, and demographic and scanner adjustment.

Results: Across the transcriptome, CT recovered at most 1.7% of expression variance (p=0.02), spread over no more than 4 of 38 genomic axes, best single gene R2 0.12. The calibrated bound on the single most decodable genomic score was 0.55 with 38 genomic components and 0.20 with only the leading 5 (43% of transcriptome variance); n=190 cannot bound it more tightly. The a-priori ccRCC panel performed no better than random gene panels (p=0.44-0.80 in 3 of 4 representations). Whole-volume deep features gave the largest single-axis signal, but 9 of their 10 best-predicted genes were sex-chromosome transcripts (TXLNGY, UTY, XIST), total recoverable variance was 0.05%, and CT decoded sex at AUC 0.81. Demographic and scanner adjustment removed 93% of it (p=0.36); signal survived in only 1 of 4 representations.

Conclusion: CT carries at most 1.7% of ccRCC transcriptome variance, and what resembles radiogenomic signal is largely sex rather than tumor biology. A cohort of this size cannot bound the per-score ceiling tightly enough to exclude a moderate effect.

Clinical Relevance Statement: CT should not be used to infer tumor genotype or defer molecular testing in renal cancer: recoverable transcriptome variance is under 2%, and the strongest apparent signal is patient sex, so such models risk encoding demographic bias rather than biology.
