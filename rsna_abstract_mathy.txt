Purpose: To establish an upper bound on how much tumor genomic information can be decoded from CT in clear-cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC), and to test whether apparent radiogenomic signal is a demographic proxy.

Materials and Methods: 190 patients from the multi-institutional Cancer Genome Atlas Kidney Renal Clear Cell Carcinoma (TCGA-KIRC) collection were analysed (median age 59 years; 65 women; 57/154 staged III-IV; 3 scanner vendors). Genomics was modeled as multivariate normal, imaging as a noisy linear readout, making the ceiling closed-form: the Bayes-optimal R2 for any genomic score is one minus its posterior-to-prior variance ratio, maximised by the squared leading canonical correlation of the whitened cross-covariance, which no decoder can exceed. Tumor RNA-seq (2000 variable genes) was paired with four CT representations (tumor and kidney pyradiomics; tumor and whole-volume RadImageNet deep features), each reduced to 38 components. Ceilings were cross-validated with permutation nulls and a patient-grouped bootstrap upper limit, then compared with an a-priori 158-gene ccRCC panel, 100 size-matched random panels, and with both modalities residualised on demographics and scanner.

Results: In-sample canonical correlations (0.56-0.69) were largely reproduced under permutation (null 0.49-0.57, 82-99% of the observed value), measuring random-matrix geometry, not recoverability. Cross-validated ceilings were far lower (0.09 tumor radiomics, p=0.015; 0.12 kidney radiomics, p=0.005; 0.06 tumor deep, p=0.10; 0.40 whole-volume deep, p=0.005), overstated in-sample by up to ninefold, with 95% upper limits 0.15-0.31 and 2-4 identifiable directions of 38. The ccRCC panel matched the full transcriptome but not size-matched random panels (p=0.44-0.80 in 3 of 4 representations), so the shared axis is not kidney-cancer specific. Imaging decoded sex (AUC 0.58-0.81) and age (R2 up to 0.09). The largest ceiling carried the strongest sex signal and retained 11% after demographic adjustment; tumor radiomics fell to 0.035 (p=0.30) once scanner was removed. Only kidney radiomics persisted (0.16).

Conclusion: Genomic information decodable from CT in ccRCC is bounded at R2 0.31 with 95% confidence across at most four directions, is not specific to kidney-cancer genes, and where largest is demographic and scanner-driven. In-sample canonical correlations overstate it up to ninefold and should not be reported as bounds.

Clinical Relevance Statement: Radiogenomic biomarkers for renal cancer risk stratification should be benchmarked against an explicit decoding ceiling and adjusted for demographics and scanner: a bound this size implies CT cannot substitute for molecular testing; unadjusted correlations overstate recoverable signal severalfold.
